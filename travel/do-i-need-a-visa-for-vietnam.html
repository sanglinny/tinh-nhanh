Do I need a visa for Vietnam? (2026 rules by passport)

Du lịch Việt NamUpdated 12/11/20265 min readEdited by Tính Nhanh

It depends on your passport. Citizens of 24 European and Asian countries, including the UK, Germany, France, Japan and South Korea, can stay up to 45 days without a visa, and most ASEAN neighbours get 14 to 30 days; everyone else, including travellers from the US, Canada, Australia, India and China, needs an e-visa, which costs US$25 for a single entry and allows up to 90 days.

45 days visa-free: 24 countries

These are one-sided exemptions granted by Vietnam's government. They do not depend on what your own country offers Vietnamese citizens, and both groups now run until 2028.

Note the second group is for tourism. If you are coming for work or business on one of those passports, ask the embassy which visa you need.

Why three countries say "check before travel"

Official lists do not agree for these passports, and we would rather tell you that than guess:

  • Indonesia: the national tourism site vietnam.travel shows 14 days; the foreign ministry list and most other sources show 30. If you are unsure, plan on 14.
  • Myanmar: the foreign ministry list shows 14 days; vietnam.travel shows 30. Plan on 14 until you confirm.
  • Belarus: the bilateral agreement for ordinary passports (in force since 30 Jan 2025) gives 30 days per entry and 90 days per year. vietnam.travel lists Belarus in the 45-day group, while KPMG reports it was dropped from Resolution 44/NQ-CP.

For any of these, ask the Vietnamese embassy or your airline before you book a stay near the limit.

Everyone else: the 90-day e-visa

Since 15 Aug 2023, citizens of every country and territory can apply for Vietnam's e-visa (Resolution 127/NQ-CP, Law 23/2023/QH15). It is also the answer if your trip is longer than your visa-free allowance.

Maximum stay90 days
EntriesSingle or multiple, chosen when you apply
Government feeUS$25 single entry, US$50 multiple entry
ProcessingAbout 3–5 working days; apply at least 1 week before you fly
Official portalsevisa.gov.vn and thithucdientu.gov.vn

A visa-free stay usually cannot be turned into a visa without leaving the country. If there is any chance you will stay longer than your exemption, get the e-visa before you arrive. The old rule requiring a 30-day gap between visa-free entries no longer applies.

Fake e-visa sites: how to spot them

Only websites ending in .gov.vn are official. Many look-alike sites with names containing "vietnam-visa" or "evisa-vietnam", often on .org or .com, charge US$50–150 or more for the same e-visa the government sells for US$25.

Type the address yourself rather than clicking a search ad. Before paying, check the address bar ends in .gov.vn and the fee is US$25 or US$50. If a site offers "express" service for several times the price, close it.

Phu Quoc: 30 days for every nationality

Phu Quoc island has its own rule. Any nationality can stay 30 days without a visa if you fly directly into Phu Quoc on an international flight and stay on the island. If you then want to go to Ho Chi Minh City or anywhere else on the mainland, you need a visa or a visa exemption that covers your passport. A domestic flight from Phu Quoc to Saigon does not count as staying on the island.

Passport validity

Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your date of entry and have at least 2 blank pages. This applies to visa-exempt travellers too, not only e-visa holders. Check the expiry date before you book, not the week you fly: a passport with less than 6 months left on your arrival date does not meet the rule.

Quick answers

  • US, Canadian, Australian, Indian or Chinese passport: not visa-exempt. Get the e-visa.
  • UK passport, 3-week trip: visa-free for up to 45 days; the policy runs to 14 Mar 2028.
  • German passport, 2-month trip: 60 days is longer than 45, so get the 90-day e-visa.
  • Dutch passport, business meetings: the Netherlands exemption is for tourism; confirm with the embassy.
